1. Restated Test Objective
The Test Objective seeks to prove two things for a sample of invoices above $5,000 processed during the audit period: (a) that both designated approvals — Finance Manager and Department Head — were formally recorded in the ERP workflow before the payment release date, and (b) that the individuals who approved held roles consistent with the authorization matrix. In effect, it tests both timeliness of approval relative to payment (sequencing) and validity of approver authority (authorization).

2. Alignment Check
Alignment to Risk: Aligned. The stated risk is that invoices are paid without proper authorization, resulting in unauthorized or fraudulent disbursements. The Test Objective directly targets the authorization mechanism by verifying that both required approvals were present before payment release and that approvers possessed the requisite delegated authority. A failure on either dimension would manifest the stated risk. No misalignment exists.

Alignment to Control Description: Aligned. The Control Description specifies a single control with two testable attributes: (1) dual approval — both Finance Manager and Department Head must approve, and (2) this must occur within the ERP workflow before payment release. The Test Objective addresses both attributes explicitly: it confirms dual approval was recorded (existence and completeness of approvals) and validates approver roles against the authorization matrix (validity of authority). Additionally, the sequencing element — approvals recorded prior to payment release date — confirms the preventive nature of the control operated as designed. All stated control components are addressed; no component remains untested by this objective.

Alignment to Audit Objective: Aligned. The Audit Objective requires assessment of whether invoice payments above the approval threshold were properly authorized. The Test Objective is bounded to exactly this scope — invoices exceeding $5,000 — and tests proper authorization through both the presence of required approvals and the legitimacy of the approvers. It neither expands scope (e.g., into invoice accuracy, duplicate payments, or vendor master integrity) nor narrows it below what the Audit Objective requires. This single test objective is sufficient to support a conclusion on the stated Audit Objective, provided the sample is representative of the full population.

3. Testability Assessment
The Test Objective is testable as written. It specifies:

A defined population: invoices exceeding $5,000 processed during the audit period
A sampling approach: explicit reference to a sample
Measurable criteria: (a) both approvals recorded in ERP workflow, (b) approval timestamps precede payment release date, (c) approver roles match the authorization matrix
Identifiable evidence sources: ERP workflow approval logs, payment release records, authorization matrix
An auditor could execute this test without ambiguity. The criteria are binary (present/absent, before/after, match/mismatch), making exception identification straightforward. No vague, circular, or aspirational phrasing is present.

One refinement opportunity (non-blocking): the objective does not specify whether "processed" means invoice posting date, payment execution date, or payment run date. This could affect population completeness depending on the ERP system's transaction lifecycle. This is a minor precision point rather than a testability deficiency.

5. Verdict
Fit for purpose. The Test Objective is traceable to the risk and control, covers all stated control components, is appropriately bounded to the Audit Objective, is testable with identifiable evidence, and complies with the methodology's requirements for operating effectiveness testing on a sample basis.

6. Suggested Revision(s)
Not required. The objective is fit for purpose. One optional precision enhancement is noted below for consideration but does not constitute a required revision:

Optional enhancement: Clarify the population definition from "processed during the audit period" to "for which payment was released during the audit period" — this eliminates potential ambiguity about whether the population anchor is invoice receipt date, posting date, or payment date, and ensures the population captures all payments at risk regardless of when the invoice was initially entered.

7. Open Questions for the Auditor
What is the defined population anchor — is "processed" measured from invoice posting date, approval date, or payment release date? This affects completeness of the test population.
Is the authorization matrix version-controlled? If roles changed during the audit period, which version of the matrix will be applied to invoices approved before versus after the change?
Does the test scope include invoices that bypassed the ERP workflow entirely (e.g., emergency payments, manual disbursements)? If such a bypass mechanism exists, a separate test objective or population completeness check may be needed to detect invoices that circumvented the dual-approval control.
Are retroactive approvals (post-payment) flagged separately in the ERP, or would they appear identical to pre-payment approvals? If the latter, the sequencing test may need to rely on timestamp granularity.
