- Objective Check Status: Not Passed

1. Restated Risk

The risk is that policies, standards, and functional rules governing agent network management (specifically IFA management) either do not exist where required, are incomplete in their coverage of applicable requirements, or fail to comply with the relevant Functional Rules, Standards, and Policies mandated by the firm. Three distinct failure modes are embedded in a single risk statement: (a) complete absence of a required policy, (b) a policy that exists but does not fully address its required scope, and (c) a policy that exists but is misaligned with or contradicts the governing Functional Rules.

2. Restated Control Description

The AzP Retail Intermediaries Team reviews and approves local policies and guidelines at least annually — or more frequently where circumstances require — to confirm that those policies are in line with relevant Functional Rules, Standards, and Policies. Evidence of each review is recorded on the documents themselves and retained by the team. The description also asserts that required policies are hosted centrally and have been communicated to team members.

3. Mitigation Assessment

Threshold Test: The first two sentences describe a control activity — a named actor (AzP Retail Intermediaries Team) performing a defined action (reviews and approves) at a stated frequency (annually or more frequently). This passes the threshold test for a control mechanism. However, the third sentence — "Required policies and guidelines are hosted in a centralised location and have been appropriately communicated to all team members as required" — is a condition statement. It asserts a desired end-state (centralised hosting, appropriate communication) with no actor performing a specific action at a defined time. This condition statement does not constitute a separate control mechanism and cannot be credited as mitigating activity.

Rating: Partially Mitigates

Failure-mode mapping:

- Non-compliant policies (failure mode c): Directly addressed. The review-and-approve cycle explicitly confirms alignment with Functional Rules, Standards, and Policies. If performed as described, this mechanism would detect and correct non-compliance before policies remain in force in a misaligned state.

- Incomplete policies (failure mode b): Indirectly and partially addressed. A review for alignment with Functional Rules could surface incompleteness if those Functional Rules specify required content. However, the Control Description does not state that completeness is an explicit review criterion — the stated objective is "to confirm they are in line with," which speaks to compliance rather than coverage scope.

- Non-existing policies (failure mode a): Not addressed. The control's action object is "local policies and guidelines" — i.e., documents that already exist. There is no stated mechanism for identifying which policies are required by Functional Rules and then detecting gaps where a required policy has not been created. A review of existing policies, however thorough, cannot surface a document that does not exist.

Scope-mismatch note: The condition statement in sentence three (centralised hosting and communication) addresses policy distribution and awareness — a legitimate operational concern, but one that sits outside the three failure modes stated in the risk. It does not contribute to the mitigation of non-existence, incompleteness, or non-compliance.

4. Control Type Consideration

- Stated type: Preventive.

- Assessment: The described mechanism — an annual (or ad hoc) review of existing policies — is detective in character. It identifies misalignment after policies have been in force, rather than preventing misaligned policies from being published in the first instance. A genuinely preventive control for this risk would typically be a pre-publication approval gate (i.e., no policy goes live until reviewed and signed off against the governing Functional Rule). The description's "reviews and approves" language could imply such a gate, but the annual frequency and absence of a triggering event (e.g., "before publication" or "upon amendment") indicate a periodic look-back rather than a real-time gate.

- Frequency inconsistency: The control metadata classifies frequency as "Transactional," implying event-driven execution. The description states "annually or more frequently where required." These are not equivalent. If the control is genuinely transactional (triggered each time a policy is created or amended), the description should say so and name the triggering event. If it is genuinely annual, the metadata should reflect a periodic frequency.

- Exposure window: If the control operates only annually, there is up to a 12-month window during which a non-compliant, incomplete, or absent policy could remain undetected — a material exposure gap for a preventive classification.

5. Specificity Assessment (4W+1H)

Per the NFRM Functional Rule, Section C.IV.2: a control description should specify what is monitored, why the control is necessary, who performs or owns it, when and how frequently it is carried out, and how it is carried out including the evidence generated.

- What (subject monitored): Present. Local policies and guidelines, reviewed for alignment with relevant Functional Rules, Standards, and Policies.

- Why (linkage to the risk): Partially Stated. The linkage to compliance/alignment is clear. The linkage to detecting non-existing or incomplete policies is not articulated — the "why" covers only one of the three failure modes in the risk.

- Who (actor/owner): Present. AzP Retail Intermediaries Team is named as the performing actor.

- When (frequency/trigger): Partially Stated. "Annually" provides a base frequency, but "more frequently where required" is open-ended — no trigger conditions are defined for the ad hoc cycle. Additionally, the stated "Transactional" metadata frequency is inconsistent with the annual description.

- How (method and evidence generated): Partially Stated. The action is described at a high level (reviews and approves; evidence recorded on documents and retained). However, the review method is not specified (e.g., comparison against a checklist derived from Functional Rules, line-by-line gap analysis). The evidence type is generic ("recorded on the documents") — no specific evidence artifact is named (e.g., a version-controlled review log, a signed approval record, a compliance checklist).

6. Verdict

Partially Mitigates. The control addresses the non-compliance failure mode but provides no mechanism for detecting non-existing policies and only implicitly touches incompleteness. The condition statement in the third sentence does not constitute an additional control activity.

7. Suggested Refinements

Structured against the gaps identified in the Specificity Assessment and Mitigation Assessment:

- Address the "non-existing" failure mode: Add a control step in which the AzP Retail Intermediaries Team maintains or references a required-policy inventory (derived from applicable Functional Rules) and, at each review cycle, confirms that a current policy exists for each required item. Without this, one-third of the stated risk has no mitigating mechanism.

- Why — expand the stated objective: Amend the purpose clause to explicitly include completeness and existence checks, not only alignment — e.g., "to confirm that all required policies exist, are complete in scope, and are in line with relevant Functional Rules, Standards, and Policies."

- When — define the ad hoc trigger: Replace "more frequently where required" with a named triggering event (e.g., "upon issuance of new or amended Functional Rules," "upon material regulatory change," or "upon publication of a new policy").

- When — resolve the frequency inconsistency: Align the control metadata (Transactional) with the description (annual), or amend the description to state the transactional trigger if the control is indeed event-driven.

- How — specify the review method and evidence: Name the method (e.g., "comparison against a compliance checklist derived from the applicable Functional Rule") and the evidence artifact (e.g., "evidenced by a dated and signed review checklist retained in [named repository]").

- Third sentence — convert the condition statement to a control activity: If centralised hosting and communication are intended as control elements, rewrite to name the actor, action, and trigger (e.g., "Upon approval, the AzP Retail Intermediaries Team publishes updated policies to [named central repository] and issues a communication to all team members via [named channel], evidenced by [named evidence]").
