
Audit Objective :-

The audit objective in the approved 2026 audit plan was:

• To assess the governance and control effectiveness of agent network management including sales steering and target setting; sales support and administration; remuneration and incentive schemes; and sales excellence, recruitment and training. The audit will focus on Independent Financial Advisor (IFA) management.

The audit will be performed as a catalogue audit.

The audit object, per the Master Audit Universe is Agent Network Management including Contact Management.

The audit work will encompass interviews with appropriate management and other staff members, as well as the review of processes and respective key documentation to assess the design and effectiveness of in scope processes and internal controls. The performance or outcome of the respective key processes and controls will be verified by means of operating effectiveness or substantive testing.

Risk :- Non-existing, Incomplete, or Non-compliant Policies, Standards and Functional Rules.

Control :- Gov 3b_Operational Structure Documentation and Procedural Knowledge / Content Expertise.

Control description:- Up-to-date documentation of operational structure (e.g., Corporate Rules/Policies, Adonis/Process descriptions, etc.)


Here is the formal Gate 1 Control Design Assessment.

1. Restated Risk

The risk posits that policies, standards, and/or functional rules governing the audited function — in this case, Agent Network Management including IFA management — either (a) do not exist where they are required, (b) exist but are incomplete in their coverage of required subject matter, or (c) exist but do not comply with applicable Allianz Group Corporate Rules, Allianz Standards, or regulatory requirements. The failure pathway operates on three distinct vectors: absence of governing documentation leaves the function without mandated operational parameters; incomplete documentation creates gaps that allow uncontrolled activity; and non-compliant documentation means the function operates under local rules that do not meet Group or regulatory standards, exposing the entity to supervisory, legal, or operational risk.

2. Restated Control Description

The control, as provided, states: documentation of the operational structure — exemplified by Corporate Rules/Policies and Adonis/Process descriptions — is maintained in an up-to-date state. No named actor responsible for maintaining or reviewing this documentation is identified. No review frequency, trigger event, or approval mechanism is stated. No completeness criteria or compliance-assessment mechanism is described.

It is noted that per the SoG Controls Guidance Paper, the full Group Audit definition of Gov_3b includes a second element: "and proper communication of the structure to all affected staff (including training sessions)." The control description as provided by the auditor omits this communication and training element.[1][2]

3. Mitigation Assessment

Rating: Partially Mitigates

The control's relationship to each of the risk's three failure modes is as follows:

- Non-existing policies/standards/functional rules: The control partially addresses this. If documentation is maintained "up-to-date," it implies documentation exists. However, maintaining "documentation of operational structure" is not equivalent to ensuring that all required policies, standards, and functional rules for Agent Network Management and IFA management are in place. The control does not include an inventory or gap assessment to confirm that no required document is absent.

- Incomplete policies/standards/functional rules: The control does not address this failure mode. No minimum content standard, completeness checklist, or coverage-assessment mechanism is described. Documentation may exist and be current yet still omit material requirements — the control as written would not detect this.

- Non-compliant policies/standards/functional rules: The control does not address this failure mode. No mechanism for assessing compliance of local policies/procedures against Allianz Group Corporate Rules, applicable Allianz Standards, or Functional Rules is described. Documentation may exist, be current, and appear complete yet still diverge from Group requirements — the control as written has no means of identifying this.

Additionally, the control description focuses on "operational structure" documentation, which is narrower in scope than the risk statement's reference to "Policies, Standards and Functional Rules." Operational structure documentation (e.g., process maps, org charts in Adonis) is a subset of, and distinct from, the full corpus of policies, standards, and functional rules that the risk contemplates.

4. Control Type Consideration

The control as described is preventive in nature — it is intended to ensure documentation is maintained in a current state, thereby preventing the condition of absent or outdated documentation from persisting. However, the risk requires both preventive and detective control elements. A preventive control would ensure required documentation is created, approved, and updated on schedule. A detective control would periodically assess whether the documentation inventory is complete and whether its content complies with Group requirements. The control as written provides only a general preventive posture (currency of existing documentation) and lacks any detective mechanism for completeness or compliance assessment. For a risk scoped to IFA management governance within a catalogue audit, the absence of a compliance-assessment element is a material gap, as non-compliance is one of the three explicitly stated failure modes.

5. Specificity Assessment

The control description is insufficiently specific for a robust design-adequacy assessment:

- No named actor — the description does not identify who is responsible for maintaining or reviewing the documentation (e.g., process owner, compliance function, distribution management).

- No defined frequency or trigger — "up-to-date" is stated as a condition but no review cadence (e.g., annual, upon regulatory change, upon process change) or trigger event is defined. This makes it impossible to assess whether the control would operate with sufficient frequency to keep pace with the risk.

- No defined scope of documentation — the parenthetical "(e.g., Corporate Rules/Policies, Adonis/Process descriptions, etc.)" is illustrative, not exhaustive. It is unclear whether this control is intended to cover all policies, standards, and functional rules relevant to Agent Network Management, or only a subset.

- No defined standard for "up-to-date" — there is no threshold or criteria for what constitutes currency (e.g., reviewed within the last 12 months, updated following each regulatory change).

- Communication element omitted — the Group Audit definition of Gov_3b includes "proper communication of the structure to all affected staff (including training sessions)," which the provided control description does not include.[1][2]

6. Verdict

Partially Mitigates — the control addresses the existence/currency dimension of the risk in general terms but does not address the completeness or compliance dimensions, and lacks the specificity required for confident design-adequacy assessment.

7. Suggested Refinements

To close the identified gaps, the control description would need to incorporate the following:

- Completeness assessment — include a periodic inventory review confirming that all policies, standards, and functional rules required for Agent Network Management / IFA management (by Group Corporate Rules and local regulation) are in place.

- Compliance assessment — include a mechanism (e.g., annual self-assessment or compliance review) to verify that the content of local policies, standards, and procedures aligns with applicable Allianz Group Corporate Rules, Allianz Standards, and Functional Rules.

- Named actor and frequency — specify who performs the review (e.g., IFA Management Head, Compliance function) and at what cadence (e.g., at least annually, and upon material regulatory or Group rule changes).

- Communication and training element — incorporate the second element of the full Gov_3b definition: proper communication of the documented structure to all affected staff, including training sessions.

- Defined scope — explicitly state which document categories fall within scope (policies, standards, functional rules, SOPs, process descriptions) rather than relying on non-exhaustive examples.

8. Open Questions for the Auditor

- Scope of "operational structure documentation": Does the entity interpret this control as covering only Adonis process maps and org-level documentation, or does it extend to all policies, standards, and functional rules relevant to Agent Network Management? If the former, the control scope is narrower than the risk and the verdict would remain unchanged. If the latter, the control description should be amended to state this explicitly.

- Companion controls: Is Gov_3b intended to be the sole control mitigating this risk, or is it one element of a broader control set (e.g., paired with a separate compliance-review control)? If paired controls exist, the partial-mitigation finding may be acceptable for this individual control, but the full risk coverage should be documented in the RACM. If Gov_3b is the sole control, the partial-mitigation verdict stands as a design gap.

- Communication and training element: Was the omission of the communication/training element from the control description intentional, or was it an oversight in transcription from the SoG Guidance Paper? If the full Gov_3b definition applies, this should be reflected in the RACM entry.

9. Methodology Compliance and References

Per the System of Governance Controls Guidance Paper (April 2026), Slide 15: Gov_3b is defined as "Operational structure documentation and Procedural knowledge/content expertise: Up-to-date documentation of operational structure (e.g., Corporate Rules/Policies, Adonis/Process descriptions, etc.) and proper communication of the structure to all affected staff (including training sessions)." Governance Controls included in the Governance Section should be tested for every audit object.[1][2]

The Guidance Paper further states that Internal Audit will independently test SoG Controls following key control testing criteria described in the Allianz Standard for Internal Audit (Standard Audit Manual, SAM).[1][2] The uploaded methodology document (Allianz UK SAM v2) does not appear to define specific control-design adequacy criteria for the purpose of this Gate 1 assessment; accordingly, the assessment above draws on general IA practice (IIA Standards, COSO) for the standard of what constitutes an adequate control description, clearly labelled as such.

10. Gate Status

Gate 1: Not Passed. The control description, as provided, only partially mitigates the stated risk — the completeness and compliance dimensions are unaddressed, and the description lacks sufficient specificity for confident design-adequacy assessment. Gate 2 (DET/OET review) is not yet available in this tool.
