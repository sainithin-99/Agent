1. Restated Test Objective

The Test Objective, as written, asks to prove that the user access management process is "working effectively" — a general assertion of operational effectiveness across the entire process. It does not identify a specific control attribute, a measurable threshold, a defined population, evidence sources, or any bounded scope tied to the stated Risk or Control Description. In plain terms, it is a restatement of the process name appended to a generic effectiveness claim.

2. Alignment Check

Alignment to Risk: Not Aligned.
The stated risk identifies a specific failure mode: terminated employees retaining system access, enabling unauthorized transactions or data. The Test Objective makes no reference to terminated employees, access deactivation, retention of access, or any mechanism by which the risk materializes. "User access management is working effectively" is broad enough to encompass provisioning, periodic reviews, privileged access, segregation of duties, and authentication controls — none of which are the stated risk. An auditor working from this objective would have no basis for targeting the specific risk of post-termination access retention.

Alignment to Control Description: Not Aligned.
The Control Description specifies two discrete control components with defined actors, timelines, and systems: (1) HR notification within 24 hours via the offboarding ticket system, and (2) IT deactivation within 1 business day. The Test Objective references none of these elements — no actor, no timeline, no system, no triggering event, and no control component. There is no traceable link between what the objective asks to be proven and what the control is designed to do.

Alignment to Audit Objective: Not Aligned.
The Audit Objective requires confirmation that access for terminated employees is removed "timely and consistently with policy." The Test Objective omits the population (terminated employees), the attribute (timeliness), the benchmark (policy-defined SLAs), and the scope boundary (access removal). While the phrase "working effectively" could theoretically encompass the Audit Objective, it does so only by being so broad that it encompasses everything and targets nothing. This is not a scoping decision — it is an absence of scope.

3. Testability Assessment

Not Testable. The Test Objective fails every criterion for testability:

- No defined population — what is being sampled or inspected is unspecified.

- No measurable threshold — "working effectively" is not a provable proposition; it cannot be confirmed or refuted without first defining what "effective" means in measurable terms.

- No evidence sources named — an auditor cannot determine what to request or inspect.

- No control attribute identified — existence, timeliness, completeness, accuracy, and authorization are all absent.

- Circular phrasing — the objective merely asserts the conclusion it is supposed to test ("working effectively") rather than stating what evidence would demonstrate effectiveness.

An auditor handed this objective could not design a test procedure, determine a sample, or identify what "pass" and "fail" look like. This is aspirational language, not a testable proposition.

4. Methodology Compliance & References

Per Allianz UK SAM v2 - Final (02-02-2025): The methodology was reviewed for prescriptive requirements regarding test objective structure. While no specific mandatory template for test objectives was identified in the retrievable sections, the general principle that audit work must be designed to address identified risks with sufficient specificity to support conclusions is a foundational element of the standard. A test objective that cannot be traced to the risk under examination does not meet this foundational requirement.

Rating: Not Compliant. Even in the absence of a prescriptive test objective template, the methodology's risk-based approach requires that testing be designed with direct reference to the specific risk and control under examination. A generic effectiveness statement without reference to the risk, control, population, or measurable criteria fails this standard.

Additionally, per general IA practice (IIA Standard 2320 — Analysis and Evaluation), audit conclusions must be based on appropriate analyses and evaluations; a test objective that does not define what is being measured cannot produce an analysis capable of supporting a conclusion. This reference is provided because the methodology does not prescribe a specific test objective structure, and is clearly labeled as general practice.

5. Verdict

Not testable as written. The Test Objective is a generic effectiveness assertion with no traceable link to the Risk, Control Description, or Audit Objective. It names no population, no threshold, no evidence, no control attribute, and no measurable criterion. It cannot serve as the basis for a test procedure and requires a complete rewrite.

6. Suggested Revision(s)

The objective requires full replacement. The following provides an example aligned to the stated inputs:

Revised Test Objective: "For a sample of employees terminated during the audit period, confirm that: (a) HR raised an offboarding notification in the ticket system within 24 hours of the termination effective date, and (b) IT disabled the employee's system access within 1 business day of notification receipt — as evidenced by HR termination records, offboarding ticket timestamps, and system deactivation logs."

Revised Test Objective: "For a sample of employees terminated during the audit period, confirm that: (a) HR raised an offboarding notification in the ticket system within 24 hours of the termination effective date, and (b) IT disabled the employee's system access within 1 business day of notification receipt — as evidenced by HR termination records, offboarding ticket timestamps, and system deactivation logs."

What changed and why:

- Replaced a generic effectiveness assertion with a specific, provable proposition tied to both control components.

- Named the population (terminated employees during audit period), the measurable thresholds (24 hours; 1 business day), the control attributes (timeliness of notification; timeliness of deactivation), and three evidence sources.

- Ensured traceability to both the Risk (post-termination access retention) and the full Control Description (both components).

- Scoped to the Audit Objective boundary (timely and consistent with policy) without expanding beyond it.

7. Open Questions for the Auditor

- Is this the only test objective planned for this control, or was a more specific version drafted elsewhere? If this wording was a placeholder or summary header rather than the intended working-level objective, the auditor should confirm the actual detailed objective in use. Verdict impact: if a detailed version exists, this review applies to the placeholder only and the detailed version should be submitted for separate assessment.

- Was the generic phrasing intentional to cover multiple controls within User Access Management (e.g., provisioning, periodic reviews, privileged access) under a single umbrella? If so, this represents a fundamental scoping error — a single test objective cannot validly address multiple distinct controls with different attributes, populations, and evidence. Each control requires its own test objective. Verdict impact: the objective would remain not testable regardless of intent, as no single proposition can be both specific enough to test and broad enough to cover the full UAM process.
