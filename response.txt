Risk :- Non-existing, Incomplete, or Non-compliant Policies, Standards and Functional Rules.

Control :- Gov 3b_Operational Structure Documentation and Procedural Knowledge / Content Expertise.

Control description:- Up-to-date documentation of operational structure (e.g., Corporate Rules/Policies, Adonis/Process descriptions, etc.)

1. Restated Risk
The risk is that the policies, standards, and functional rules governing agent network management (specifically IFA management) may be non-existing, incomplete, or non-compliant with applicable requirements. The failure pathway is a governance-framework gap: if the normative documents that set requirements for the audited process are absent, do not cover the full scope of the process, or do not conform to higher-order regulatory or group requirements, then operational activities proceed without an authoritative and compliant basis — exposing the entity to regulatory, conduct, and operational failures.

2. Restated Control Description
The control description states: "Up-to-date documentation of operational structure (e.g., Corporate Rules/Policies, Adonis/Process descriptions, etc.)"

As written, this is an assertion that documentation of operational structure exists and is current. No actor is named. No action verb describes what someone does to create, review, approve, or maintain this documentation. No trigger, frequency, or method is specified. No evidence type is identified.

3. Mitigation Assessment
Threshold Test — Condition Statement vs. Control Activity: The control description is a condition statement, not a control activity. It asserts a desired end-state — that documentation is "up-to-date" — without identifying any actor performing any defined action at any trigger or frequency. There is no action verb (no "reviews," "updates," "approves," "validates"). Because the description names no actor and no action, the verdict ceiling under the threshold test is Does Not Mitigate, regardless of any topical overlap with the risk.

Formal Rating: Does Not Mitigate.

Failure-pathway mapping:

Even setting aside the threshold-test ceiling, the control description does not close the risk's failure pathway for the following reasons:

Topical misalignment. The risk concerns policies, standards, and functional rules — the normative governance documents that set binding requirements. The control description references "documentation of operational structure" with parenthetical examples that include "Corporate Rules/Policies" alongside "Adonis/Process descriptions." Operational structure documentation (org charts, process maps, procedural manuals) is a different category from the normative framework of policies, standards, and functional rules. The parenthetical mention of "Corporate Rules/Policies" does not establish that the control systematically addresses the existence, completeness, and compliance of all policies, standards, and functional rules within scope — it merely lists them as one example among several types of documentation.
No mechanism addresses "non-existing." The risk includes the possibility that required policies or standards do not exist at all. A statement that existing documentation is "up-to-date" does not detect or prevent the absence of documents that should exist but do not.
No mechanism addresses "non-compliant." The risk includes the possibility that policies or standards exist but do not comply with applicable higher-order requirements. Nothing in the control description describes a compliance check or benchmarking exercise against regulatory or group requirements.
No mechanism addresses "incomplete." The risk includes the possibility that policies or standards exist but are incomplete. Nothing in the control description describes a completeness assessment.
Part of the risk left unaddressed: All three failure modes — non-existence, incompleteness, and non-compliance of policies, standards, and functional rules.

Part of the control addressing something other than the stated risk: The reference to "Adonis/Process descriptions" and general "operational structure" documentation introduces scope that is not directly responsive to the governance-framework risk as stated.

4. Control Type Consideration
The control description cannot be meaningfully classified as preventive or detective because no control activity is described. If a control activity were present — for example, a periodic completeness and compliance review of the policy framework against a defined register — it would function as a detective control (identifying gaps after they arise). Alternatively, a mandatory review-and-approval gate before policies are published could be preventive (ensuring compliance before the policy takes effect). The risk's failure pathway would benefit from both: a preventive mechanism ensuring new or revised policies meet compliance standards before issuance, and a detective mechanism periodically confirming that all required policies exist and remain current and compliant. As written, neither type is present.

5. Specificity Assessment (4W+1H)
Per the NFRM Functional Rule, Section C.IV.2: a control description should specify what is monitored, why the control is necessary, who performs or owns it, when and how frequently it is carried out, and how it is carried out including the evidence generated.

What (subject monitored): Partially Stated — "documentation of operational structure" is named with parenthetical examples, but the scope is not precisely bounded. It is unclear whether the control is intended to cover all policies, standards, and functional rules relevant to the audited process, or only a subset of operational documentation.
Why (linkage to the risk): Absent — no stated rationale linking this control to the risk of non-existing, incomplete, or non-compliant policies, standards, and functional rules.
Who (actor/owner): Absent — no individual, role, or function is named as responsible for performing or owning this control.
When (frequency/trigger): Absent — "up-to-date" implies ongoing currency but specifies no frequency, trigger event, or review cycle.
How (method and evidence generated): Absent — no method is described for how documentation is maintained, reviewed, or validated. No evidence type is named. This gap is particularly relevant for future DET/OET readiness: without a named evidence type, there would be nothing for an effectiveness test to inspect.
Summary: The control description satisfies zero of the five elements fully. One element (What) is partially stated. Four elements (Why, Who, When, How) are entirely absent. This level of vagueness renders the description untestable independent of the mitigation-alignment question.

6. Verdict
Does Not Mitigate. The control description is a condition statement, not a control activity. It names no actor, no action, no trigger, no method, and no evidence. Its subject matter ("operational structure documentation") does not clearly or systematically address the risk's three failure modes (non-existence, incompleteness, and non-compliance of policies, standards, and functional rules).

7. Suggested Refinements
The control description should be rewritten as a control activity — with a named actor performing a defined action — and structured to address each missing 4W+1H element:

What: Define precisely which documents are within scope — e.g., all policies, standards, and functional rules applicable to agent network management / IFA management — rather than referencing "operational structure documentation" generically.
Why: State the control's purpose in relation to the risk — e.g., to ensure that the governance framework for the audited process is complete, current, and compliant with applicable group and regulatory requirements.
Who: Name the responsible actor or role — e.g., the Compliance function, the process owner, or a named governance role.
When: Specify a trigger or frequency — e.g., annually, upon regulatory change, or upon issuance of new group functional rules — rather than the unquantified "up-to-date."
How: Describe the method — e.g., a completeness check against a defined policy register, a compliance assessment against applicable higher-order requirements, a formal review-and-approval workflow — and name the evidence generated (e.g., a signed-off review protocol, a gap analysis report, an updated policy register with version control).
These refinements remain at the description level and do not prescribe business-process redesign.
